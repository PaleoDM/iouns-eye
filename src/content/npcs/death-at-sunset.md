A great green dragon who once held the wood now called the Gloaming, and the reason that wood is dying.

The party learned of her through a memory the great alder gave to Indri. Five flying longboats, each with a spearhead at the prow and sailing fins down its flanks, ramming themselves into her hide. The first four shattered apart against her and rained down across the Gloaming, which is where its scattered giant-wrought wreckage comes from. The fifth went in true, burying itself between the collarbone and the back wing in the one place she was not armored. Dragon and longship came down together into a clearing a few hours north of the alder, through the untamed part of the wood where no path goes.

She has been dead for centuries. Her carcass is the source of *the sickening*: a green dragon's venom, in that quantity, left where it fell, seeping outward ever since. The Gloaming is still being killed by a dragon that died long before anyone in Glancoed was born.

## The Black Box

The party reached her crash clearing in Session 3 and found the fifth longship prow-down in frozen earth, with a road-wide furrow beside it where something vast had dragged itself under. Indri salvaged the ship's black box, a garnet crystal long since ground to a coarse sand, and it still held what the fire giant crew saw in their last minutes.

It corrected the story the alder told. She did not break off that fight because she was beaten. She was protecting a clutch of eggs, and she chose them. The giants brought her down directly over her own lair, and they never actually finished her. She crawled home to die inside it.

One of them survived her. **Sunset-Is-Nigh** is weeks old and living somewhere below.

The crew's killing shot was never fired. An **Ancient Fire Giant Harpoon of Dragonslaying** is still cradled aboard the wreck, still cocked, still charged, and still aimed straight down into the wound. Indri knows how to fire it and not much else about it.

## Hearth and Hatchlight, Session 4

The killing shot finally fired, two hundred and twelve years late, and it killed her son. Sunset-Is-Nigh died pinned under the harpoon, and then the fifth longship itself tore loose and came down on top of him. The hull that killed the mother now buries the child.

Her carcass is still down there, and it is still the source. The sickening did not die with her line.
