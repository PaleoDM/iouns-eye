The gentle, petty little brook-spirit that guards the running spring the village of Glancoed drinks from. Not the lake-monster of legend: an otter-sized creature with a beaver-flat tail, a long gharial snout, and great drooping catfish whiskers, immensely dignified and immensely put-upon. The villagers have never really met it and only ever address the clumsy statue at its roadside shrine, which it finds deeply insulting.

When the travelers chased a stray hen named Priscilla to the spring, they found the shrine neglected and its offering bowl empty, and the afanc holding the hen for ransom. It was cross, and underneath that, frightened: Eirlys, the one villager who tended its shrine and sang its morning song, had been gone three days. The party cleaned the shrine, set down an offering, and Laeryn called out to it in Primordial until it deigned to surface and speak. They struck a bargain to bring the village children for a water-cycle lesson, complete with an interpretive dance from Bringha, and it gave Priscilla back.

The party made good on the bargain the following morning. The children came down and cleaned the shrine properly, Indri led the lesson on the water cycle, and Bringha danced the majesty of water at length. It was during that lesson that the party first noticed the cold crystalline light at the treeline of the Gloaming.

## Hearth and Hatchlight, Session 4

The Afanc's boon is the reason Laeryn is alive. When the harpoon's blast caught him for exactly enough to kill him outright, the boon flared on its own and bought him the one hit point between unconscious and dead. The petty little spirit of a village spring reached all the way into a dragon's lair to keep its end of a bargain.
