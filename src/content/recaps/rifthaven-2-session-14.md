We opened in the poachers' den, with the crew asleep on the poachers' own bedrolls. Olivier had spent the evening nursing Kalish, and with some food and water in him, Kalish started to talk. The camp Livid described was a couple of quarries over, built across ledges on the cliff. The matron of the circle holds court in a cavern on one of the upper layers. There were hens kept in pens, and one of their mates was close to the end of his tears. Kalish figured the druids would take that hen's chicks the moment he died. Scouts watch the cliffs, so climbing down meant being seen. The skiff would not be subtle, but it would be direct.

Astrid explained that there is a single punishment for poachers. Kalish found that terrifying until somebody told him she was the new host of the show. Then he decided it was an on-screen bit, and told Olivier it was ratings gold. Rivals test better with a name, he said. Astrid gave him one, the Circle of the Broken Tusk.

Kalish laid out two ways in. They could rappel down the cliffs under cover and hope the scouts missed them, or they could take the skiff. Nobody on the crew has ever been good at sneaking. The druids were expecting their own skiff back, so its return should not raise any alarm. They took the skiff. Olivier and Kalish went into a chest with a pillow. Astrid navigated, and Ralo drove standing on a crate. The skiff is an air gondola, built for hopping between communities along the Rift. It was never meant to set down on land.

They came in from the east. The poachers were keeping camp, and one was butchering a carcass for a kept beast. Another waved at the skiff coming home. An azer whittling a spear out of a long bone looked up and saw the wrong man driving.

Ralo still had the speed to ram, and nobody stopped him. Everyone braced. He tried to drift the gondola and sweep two poachers with its back end, turned way too hard, and put the stern into the wall. The lizardfolk mage took the keel in the chest and died, and the azer went down hard. The crew went over the side into the middle of camp, and the skiff lost its back end. It was the hardest single hit of the campaign, and a fair amount of it landed on his friends. Ralo had not felt so young since he crashed his father's carriage.

Garran caught the fallen azer before he could get up and left him barely alive. Lucien hit him with fire, which did nothing at all, so he turned it on the other lizardfolk mage. Astrid climbed onto a ledge for the high ground and shot that one dead. The lizardfolk boss charged across camp at Lucien and Ralo. Lucien's magic surged, and his skin turned to stone. The boss tried to bite Ralo and could not get through the wreckage he was wearing. Then the big azer dropped a fireball on all of them, his own people included. Garran took the worst of it. In the second round Ralo finished the wounded azer, and Lucien's lightning bolt killed another and left the big one smoking.

Astrid wanted his fire out, so she hit him with Gust of Wind. It blew him over the edge of the cliff. He caught the ledge, lost his shield to the drop, and spent everything he had climbing back up. Then the wind took him over again, and that time he stayed gone. Ralo frightened the lizardfolk boss half to death, and the rest of the crew chipped at it until it was standing at the edge. Milo finished it with a starry wisp, and it fell off the cliff too. The whole fight lasted about eighteen seconds.

Astrid put up her aura of healing, and Olivier climbed out of the chest to help. The crew lined up and got patched back together. Ralo said he did not deserve any help after that stunt, and took it anyway.

A druid came to the edge of the next ledge up and looked down at his dead friends and the broken skiff. "We have no known enemies. Who goes there?"

Astrid told him poaching is the line, and they could break any other law they liked. The druid said they use nature's resources, and asked if she had never eaten fruit from a tree. Astrid said eating fruit does not interfere with the tree, and they had to step in to make this happen. Lucien added that it was like a blood orange. Milo had never heard of one, and he was still thinking about an orange full of blood when the druids fell back. They had the high ground, and they meant to make the crew come up.

Astrid sent Pocket up for a look and watched through his eyes. There were only two druids on the pens ledge, the one who had shouted and another lizardfolk mage. They also had two owlbears, kept the way the cloaker was kept. One was gnawing a bone. The other was enormous, and it was coughing up a pellet the size of a goat. There were six pens of widow cranes, three hens with chicks, and a male who did not have long left. The druids were pointing at him and arguing about putting him down early. Then a hawk came out of the sky at Pocket, and Astrid called him home. Somebody up there has a familiar too. The distillery and the inner sanctum are further up the cliff, and the news had not reached them yet.

Owlbears are not beasts, so talking them down was out. Lucien wanted to lure them down instead, and Olivier coached him as the resident bird expert. Lucien settled on the sound of a goat. Ralo hid in a crate. The big owlbear came over the edge with the druid riding on its back, and the small one and the lizardfolk mage came after.

Ralo came out of the crate and cut the big owlbear, and it could barely land a swing after that. It got one into him anyway as he backed off. Milo slowed the small owlbear and the lizardfolk. The druid answered with a thunderwave that blew Milo off the dock. He did a little vampire twirl in the air and caught the edge with both hands. Garran ran the druid through twice. Lucien held his lightning bolt until Astrid was clear, then put it through both owlbears and the lizardfolk. His magic surged and drained a little life out of the druid and an owlbear. Astrid shot the druid dead, then backed up and shot the lizardfolk dead too.

That left the owlbears. The big one let out a screech that knocked Garran and Lucien senseless. We leave our scoundrels there, with two owlbears to go.
