We opened in Marilissa's chamber, with our heroes spent and a dragon somewhere below. They took what rest the place allowed. Yu put a cure wounds into Eirlys and watched the color come back into her face. Bringha remembered the parcel from her sensei and handed it over. Tsira had sent ankheg milk. Poison at full strength, a powerful tranquilizer diluted. A message came with the bottle, but Eirlys slipped it into her sleeve and kept it for herself.

Nobody pretended a straight fight was on the table. The harpoon pointed at one spot, and the whole plan was getting him to stand in it. Bringha got the job Eirlys called the troll beneath the bridge. Draw him out, keep him talking. Eirlys used her last spell slot to grant Bringha poison resistance.

Up top, Indri and Laeryn went to work on the harpoon. It would still fire. But centuries of freeze and thaw had it wedged in place, so any real aiming was out. Then a voice spoke out of the wreckage in Giant, calm and grandfatherly. It asked the little sprite of a thing at its controls for her name (Indri). The ship has a computer. He manifested as an illusion of a six-armed man, every arm working an abacus, hard at work doing computations. Doll-sized to a giant, which is nearly Indri-sized. He answers to Slate. His pilots, Captain Valira and Lieutenant Caleese, had kept him heard and not seen. He tried to count how long he had been alone and got stuck in a computing loop partway through. With both pilots dead, two directives remained. "Serve the ship at all costs, and fucking kill drakes." Slate offered to mesh with Indri's nervous system to steady the shot. Indri went all in without a second thought. Her eyes went rose quartz, her mind got faster, and her judgment got worse. And the harpoon carries a detonator, which he armed himself two hundred and twelve years ago.

Down below, something came up the tunnel demanding to know who had broken into his home and killed his pet. Sunset-Is-Nigh walked into the chamber vain, curious, and starved for someone to talk to. Bringha gave him exactly that. She asked after his mother. Her name was Death at Sunset, and she razed villages, towns, and once an entire city. She asked how he got his name. His mother whispered it into his head before she died, one name for a whole clutch. He ate his siblings to be the only one left to claim it. She told him dragon jokes, and they landed. He liked her enough to offer her Marilissa's old job. Bringha told him he would have to catch her first, little worm, and we rolled initiative.

The banter had done its work. Laeryn had spent the conversation drifting into position, and Harsk had melted into the rocks. Yu offered her help up the rope and got a look for it. She was the town's witch, and she was not leaving. Her own attempt to hide behind a stalactite fooled nobody. Yu opened from hiding with a fire bolt, max damage again. Bringha held a dodge. Up top, Indri and Slate worked the firing solution two ticks easier. Laeryn missed with a javelin and planted himself in the target square. The wyrmling spat at Eirlys about the antivenom she owed him and charged Laeryn. Exactly as intended.

The wyrmling charged Laeryn, and Laeryn had put himself there on purpose. In the cockpit, Indri asked Slate whether Laeryn would survive the blast. With her judgment meshed away, she could not tell. It was their one shot, Laeryn was the hardiest of them, and Yu still had healing left. The dragon was drawing poison breath over Laeryn when she fired. The roll broke thirty, and Slate offered her a choice. Soften the blast around Laeryn, or drive the harpoon into something vital and double the damage. She doubled it. If the dragon lived, they were all dead anyway. The harpoon went through the top of his skull and stapled it into the creek below. One hundred and forty damage. The splash caught Laeryn for thirty-five, exactly enough to kill him outright. The Afanc's boon flared on its own and bought him the hit point between unconscious and dead.

The blast put all five of them somewhere else for a moment. Whether it was dying or dreaming, nobody could say. Bringha knelt on an ashen plain before an immense man veined with magma. He told her she was made for more than this and seared something into her forearm. Indri never left the ship. She danced through its systems with Slate while it fell, and there was nowhere she would rather have been. Yu sat as a hummingbird at a grand candlelit table. An old voice full of pain promised him that if he wore the hat, his hummingbirds would come. Harsk stood the deck of his own ship in far southern waters and got whacked with a stick by the smallest granny in the world. Everything finds its way to the ocean, she told him. Laeryn shared a tavern table with a man wearing his own face, and promised to find what broke the world and fix it.

They came to minutes later. The ship had torn loose and come down through the opening, burying the dragon under the hull that killed his mother. The three lights blasted out through the opening above and scattered into the world in three directions. Nobody was dead. Bringha had hauled Laeryn clear with reach she did not have yesterday. Yu spent his first divine spark getting him conscious. Every one of them now bore a glowing mark, somewhere between a tattoo and a brand, Eirlys included. Slate had survived by compressing himself onto a sliver of crystal, shedding memories to fit. Indri promised to build him a new body, and he asked her not to make it a spider. Eirlys dusted herself off and asked what you are supposed to do with a green dragon. You wait until it ripens.

The lair told them who they had killed. Sunset-Is-Nigh was about twenty years old, and he had never once been outside. His mother had impressed a fear of the harpoon on him, and that fear kept him underground his whole life. He kept a shrine to her around one of her teeth, and on it sat two giant-cut striking runes. The hoard held a thousand gold, a potion of fire breath, and a dust of disappearance he never worked out. They took the tooth, the scales, and the bones.

The party made camp and roasted pears over the fire. Then Laeryn said he had not been entirely honest with them. Fifty years ago a man named Laeryn Vard made his name adventuring, then settled down and spent his life protecting a village he built in the woods. Dying, he asked a trusted friend to carry his name so the protection would outlive him. That friend was this Laeryn's father. The son wanted the name to do more than guard duty, so Laeryn Vard came out of retirement. His skin flickered and settled into a younger face nobody at the fire had seen. "My name is Rasa." A changeling. Bringha welcomed not-Laeryn to the table without missing a beat. Harsk said he needed to think on it. Yu's surprise was different. He had felt the Everlight through this man, and the real Laeryn had been hers. Rasa asked them to keep the secret for the village's sake. Bringha asked whether he had a face of his own underneath. He did not. It kind of just goes away, he said.

When the fire burned low, Eirlys finally opened the message from her sleeve. It came from Dr. Riddenour, a researcher with an estate south of Aberblaidd, carried quietly by the Coven of Whispers. Eirlys was a sister of the coven, not a member. The letter named the light. Hatchlight, they were calling it. Some of the exposed manifested strange powers. Others blighted, black veins climbing the arm and into the shoulder. The doctor was gathering the exposed at her estate to study it. Eirlys had written to her about one light. Five marked people at this fire had now seen three. They now had an invitation to their next destination.

We leave our heroes camped in a dead dragon's lair, fed on roasted pears and telling jokes about the day nobody died. Eirlys measured a drop of ankheg milk into her wine, downed it, and slept like a stone. The sickening is not finished. But Death at Sunset is.
