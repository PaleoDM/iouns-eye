We opened cold, before we ever met our heroes.

A luxury fighting arena. The rich and the elite up in the boxes, gambling and politicking over their drinks while the crowd roared below. Outside, in the same afternoon light, a young boy was being hanged. Petty larceny, theft, general skulduggery. Rifthaven's regular business. A young scoundrel watched it happen, and an older, wiser hand watched beside him. The boy had been fast, fast enough that nobody should have caught him. The old scoundrel had a different read. "You've always got to stick to the plan, lad. And always bloody communicate. You can't go thinking you're bigger than the crew."

Two rules. The boy on the rope learned'em the hard way.

From there we cut to our heroes, one at a time.

Garran was drinking with a mutual acquaintance, a dwarf with an orange beard and hair named Bobilliath, Bobbi for short. They traded light banter and then got to business, chewing over the strange arcane contraption that had come up out of the Rift, the one that can forge a man's handwriting perfectly. Both of them needed work. Bobbi had heard a job was going down at a bathhouse, the Brimstone Bath, though he couldn't say whether the job was happening there or whether that was just where you went to hear about it. They decided to work it on a buddy system and go together.

Astrid was at her day job, tending her toucans and her chinchillas. Her neighbor came in, a gnomish woman named Celeste who owns the bodega across the way. Celeste talked down to her a little, the way she always does, because she has no idea Astrid's divination is the real thing. Celeste is an astrology junkie. She thinks Astrid's whole act is a sad imitation of her own. She did pass along news worth having. The College of Divination was bleeding members, enough that her own accepted place had to wait. One of the professors had gone missing. Celeste was convined that finding that professor would be her entrance exam. Meanwhile, Astrid was reading a message about a job down at the Brimstone Bath...

Milo was nursing a hangover he is not at all used to. Five centuries a vampire and now he has to drink water like everybody else. His manservant, who insists on being called only Thrall, was there to suffer it with him. The two of them bickered about the water and about the old life neither of them has quite let go. Eventually the talk turned to a job, also at the Brimstone Bath. Milo agreed to go, on the condition that Thrall go ahead and arrange for a patron to invite him in. He does not enter a place uninvited. He told Thrall to go on while he counted to a thousand, who pointedly reminded him that last time Milo tried to count to a thousand he became uncomfortably aware of his own heartbeat. There was a small tantrum.

Lucien was in a more genteel tavern, very well dressed, when a former colleague found him. Sage. Elegant, beautiful, every hair in place. She had a lead she did not care to work herself, the kind of thing she will not dirty her sleeves on. An elderly woman named Nonna Battaglia had come to her about a legal challenge, a mutually agreed separation involving her adult son. Sage was loath to touch it because the Battaglia boy is tangled up with the da Pinci family and she does not meddle in those things. Lucien cut straight to the real shape of the problem. What the boy actually needed was help with his brand and his identity, and that is precisely Lucien's wheelhouse. Sage was glad to have handed it to someone who got it. The Battaglia boy would be waiting at the Brimstone Bath.

And Ralo was in his mother's kitchen, mostly arguing. She wanted a new tennis bracelet and a son who provided better. He pointed out that his four brothers still live at home and contribute nothing. They got into his estranged wife, his petulant little shit of a son, when you follow a recipe and when you don't. They settled nothing. Ralo was livid to learn his Nonna had been soliciting lawyers on his behalf behind his back. She begged and begged him to at least go meet the man at the Brimstone Bath. He reluctantly agreed.

So they all came to the same room, for different reasons. Garran, Astrid, and Milo were there chasing the job. Ralo and Lucien were there for the other business entirely.

Garran and Astrid fell in with Bobbi over a drink, Astrid handling the talking, since communication is her specialty. Demolition too, on occasion, but mostly communication. That is the bread and butter. Across the room, Lucien tried to open his pitch to Ralo and it went nowhere. Ralo had no interest in being represented. He was, however, delighted to report that he had seen Lucien's advertisement in the urinal at Bennigan's, and that the likeness was fantastic, and that the ad clearly worked because here he was remembering the product. Lucien could not tell if he was being trolled. He did not enjoy it either way. About then Thrall arrived and asked Astrid to invite his master in, which she did, and Milo made his entrance.

Then our quest-givers walked in. The Sorvellans, father and son, Kelvaryn and Vendris. The bouncers seemed to be expecting them, and for a moment it looked like the two of them were about to get roughed up. Our heroes moved to intervene. Milo and Lucien are the charming ones, but it was Garran who stepped up, cajoling and leaning on the bouncers until they backed down. That brought out the proprietor herself. Cobalt, a tiefling who runs a tight, neutral house in the turf wars. She recognized Ralo and assumed he was there to pull a job on her. There was a great deal of arguing. It came out that the Sorvellans were simply idiots who hadn't known they were supposed to pay the house to host their meeting. Cobalt had heard there was a job at the Brimstone Bath and, like everyone else, couldn't tell whether that meant a job against the Brimstone Bath. She'd had her bouncers ready. Once the misunderstanding cleared, a couple of coins changed hands, Astrid's coins as it happened, and the party got one of the quiet private rooms on the side.

There the Sorvellans laid out the work. Documents of a personal nature, written by the young master in youthful indiscretion, taken by a woman who had been close to him when she had been his private tutor. They need to be recovered before they reach unkind hands. Kelvaryn would not say much, but he gave up enough. The documents are politically a bad look, and they cannot be allowed to surface, with him set to step into his family's council seat. The woman who took them goes by La Marginalia, though no one offered a real name behind it. Beyond that he held the line. And he was firm on one rule. The documents, bearing his own seal, must be returned before they hit the broadsheet printers. That was the line that made the whole thing feel sensitive. Lucien was in his element, talking through how he'd draft the contract so every term landed just right.

Then the haggling. The Sorvellans offered a hundred gold a head. Our heroes countered at three hundred. They settled at two hundred a head, which, as Garran quietly reminded everyone, is a staggering sum for a crew that is level three. "Holy shit, we have to say yes to that." So naturally they called a huddle and made a show of driving a hard bargain, and in the middle of that huddle, faces close together, they introduced themselves to one another for the first time. Then they took the job.

The Sorvellans lingered, neither willing to be the first one out the door. Lucien finally escorted them. They got as far as the back alley before someone tried to mug them, and our heroes had to step in one more time. Lucien dropped a well-placed Suggestion. Garran and Milo backed it with some genuinely frightening intimidation. Astrid laid down heavy supporting fire on the first round, and Ralo wrecked the man running the whole thing. It did not last past the first round. Several of the muggers turned tail and ran, the Sorvellans were safe, and that was that.

Our heroes walked back into the Brimstone Bath to finish their game of cards and actually get to know each other before the real work begins. Tomorrow they pick up La Marginalia's trail and follow it to the Halworth Reading Rooms, hoping it leads them to the young master Sorvellan's missing papers.

A crew of strangers, two hundred gold a head, and a job that nobody will explain. Not a bad first day.
