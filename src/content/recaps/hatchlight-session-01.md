We opened with the monologue, just as planned.

> Before Kalari. Before Ishetar. Before Dirk Adventureman or Rifthaven. Before Astoria as we've come to know it. The Second Age was… just a little more wild. Equal parts Cozy and Chaos.
>
> The land thrums with residual arcana from the myriad Great Wyrms. Leylines swell with primordial power connecting deep elemental wells. And Giant-wrought machines carve across the landscape.
>
> And amidst it all, the small folk do as they ever have: they persist. Armed only with Heart and with Hearth, they flourish between the scars of conflict much larger than they.
>
> And there is no better place to set our story than the small forest village of Glancoed. It is here that an elderly grandmother prepares the evening meal for a group of travelers. And here that villagers have spotted mysterious lights in the forest, known to them in the common tongue as "The Gloaming."
>
> But I'm getting a bit ahead of myself, for our travelers' stories actually begin just a little… bit… earlier.

Then we handed off to five travelers, each on their own road into a village called Glancoed, ahead of a storm.

We started with Bringha. We found her in a dojo, black and red robes everywhere, a room full of bodies moving in perfect sync. Bringha was...not moving in perfect sync. She was flamboyant, off-script, modifying the set moves as she pleased, and her sensei was done with it. No discipline. Exasperated, the sensei handed her a parcel and told her to deliver it to a village called Glancoed, and that was the push out the door. Taped to the parcel was a note: "just because the mighty elm can sway in the breeze does not mean it does not also need to put down firm roots."

Then Harsk. He dragged a buck he had killed into Glancoed on a half-finished sled and went to barter with the local butcher. The butcher did not haggle, mostly out of mercy, because Harsk is genuinely bad at it. They talked over whether the kill might rise again, the way things do in this age, and Harsk assured him that Indri was on her way to handle exactly that. The butcher brightened at the news that Indri was coming, told Harsk to go find some ale, and to get out of the storm rolling in.

Then Laeryn. He was in a nice house in a small village, packing to leave, when another Laeryn walked in. The two of them argued it out, one trying to talk the other out of going. Can a man do more good out in the world, or at home where he stands? It went back and forth and it got heartfelt, until Laeryn admitted the real reason. He was going because he wanted to be more than Laeryn. That was all the other one needed to hear. He gave his blessing. And Laeryn changed, the elven figure becoming a dwarven one, and slipped out the north side of the village onto the road.

Then Indri. She was packing a bag with no clothes and no food, only gadgets, doohickeys, and scraps of salvage, all of it somehow fitting into a space that should not have held it. She came out to the kitchen table to say goodbye to her parents. Her father was bent over a small contraption with a scroll open beside him, and a strange blight had blanched one of his arms pale all the way up to the collarbone. Indri announced she was packed. Her father had lost track of the days and thought she was leaving tomorrow, then teased her mother that you cannot argue with a packed bag. He asked if she even knew where she was going. Indri produced a compass. Her father worked a small illusion over it and changed the rune from "North" to "Adventure". Big hugs. Her mother's last word to her urging her to mind her manners.

Then Yu. He walked into the local courier, because of course, there is a Pony Express in this world. Yu paid to ship a modest parcel of money off to Dolgaer, a tithe to his church, Raei the Everlight. He sheepishly paid for insurance on top, because he is a trustworthy person. On the way out a begging child stopped him and pushed back on the whole idea, skeptical that money does more good piled up in a faraway city when the post office skims its cut. Yu held his ground. Pooled resources go farther. The kid heard him out, then walked him off to see whether he could fix his little brother's broken toy. And from there, we arrived at long last, in Glancoed.

The storm closed the roads behind them and the village gathered everyone under one roof for a meal. Mamgu Nerys was at the pot, cooking up a stew, flirting with Harsk and asking whether he might make her a wedding band out of the buck's antlers. Then she put him to work, sending him to round up the children clustered around Yu and herd them over to Indri for a lesson. Indri obliged, painting bright little spirits and flourishes onto a set of bones, coaxing the dead to remember what they had been so the last of the ambient magic would burn off and they would stay down. Laeryn stepped in to help a local shore up against the storm wall, and the man mentioned he was nearly out of nails and would need iron soon. That thread got cut short when Bringha knocked over a keg of honey, the chicken coop banged open, and every last hen made a break for it.

The heroes went after the birds. Bringha catapulted herself off a bench toward one stuck up in the rafters, overshot, smacked her head on the roof, took no damage at all, and caught the chicken clean on the way down. Indri raised the bones of an old sheepdog and reminded it of its training, and it herded a hen neatly back into the pen. Laeryn used a little leadership and three encouraged children to triangulate another. Yu went gentle, kneeling down and coaxing his hen with patience and kind words until it let him scoop it up. It clawed him up for his trouble anyway, and Bringha swooped in to help him get the scratched-up bird back into the coop. Harsk still had Speak with Animals going and got into it with a hen named Gertrude, who was deeply smitten with him and wanted him to know she was not like the other hens, while he sheepishly shooed her back inside. They counted up. There should have been eight. They had five. Three had bolted into the storm.

The tracks of two led down toward the road out of town. They were closing in when the storm spat out four little spirits of ice and spite and the fight was on. Indri opened with a max-damage fire bolt that vaporized one outright and told everyone exactly what these things did not like. Then it got ugly. A spirit caught Bringha, Laeryn, and Harsk with frost breath and hurt them both. Laeryn whiffed a couple of swings, but distracted one entirely in the meantime. A second spirit breathed on Yu and dropped him in one shot. He failed his death save, and in that moment Yu wondered why he could not feel the Everlight, his own goddess, anywhere near him at the edge of death. Laeryn got healing into him and pulled him back up, and Bringha, Indri, and Harsk closed it out with the fire the spirits feared. On his feet again, Yu immediately asked who else needed healing. The party told him to heal himself. Two chickens recovered, the new companions talked it over as they walked toward the spring where the last one had gone.

Yu got himself back to full with a Cure Wounds. At the spring they found Priscilla, the last hen, bobbing in the water like a duck, beside a shrine to the local spring spirit that had gone neglected, its offering bowl empty. Harsk, still talking to animals, got the story out of Priscilla. The spirit was cross and holding her for ransom over how the village had treated it. Laeryn called out in Primordial, rolled well, and the spirit rose up to answer him in kind. The Afanc, a cross between a beaver and a gharial with long, catfish like whiskers and a beaver tail, made its appearance. While they spoke, the others got to work on the shrine. Indri and Yu mended the broken stone, Bringha swept, and Harsk set down a proper offering. The spirit thawed a little and told them the rest. It was angry because Eirlys, the village witch, had been gone three days, and the offerings had stopped with her. The party worked out a deal. In the morning they would bring the village children down to the shrine for an impromptu lesson on the water cycle, complete with an interpretive dance from Bringha about the majesty of water. That was enough. The spirit gave Priscilla back.

We landed back at the longhouse, over Mamgu's stew, with Gwenllian the headwoman. She confirmed it. Eirlys had walked into the Gloaming three days ago and had not come out. The Gloaming is the forest at the village's edge, where strange lights have been seen, thick with ambient magic that bleeds off a wrecked old giant machine somewhere in its heart. Gwenllian asked them plainly to go in and find her. In return, Glancoed would give them a permanent roof, warm beds, hot meals, and whatever treasure they could carry out. They agreed, each for their own reasons, Bringha for the elementals and Indri for the old machinery. They took a long rest at the hearth and came up to Level 2.

We leave our heroes warm and fed for one more night. Come morning there is a water cycle to explain, a dance to perform, and then the treeline of the Gloaming waiting at the edge of the world.
