We opened with our scoundrels walking up on their new property in the Inkwell district, a lively stretch of the city full of music, artisans, and odd little bohemian shops. The building is five stories tall and was locked up tight from the outside. Nobody in this crew is a proper lockpick, but Ralo worked the handle until it gave and let them in.

Astrid sent a messenger off to a local smith to change the locks and cut new keys. While they waited, the crew started on the first floor. Somebody had been squatting here, and not just sleeping rough. Whoever it was had been running jobs out of the place. As they dug through it, they tripped a defense the old owner had left behind, and a set of mannequins came to life to deal with the "intruders."

Ralo and Garran took the biggest one head on while Astrid hung back and worked from range. Milo caught another with Dissonant Whispers and sent it skittering across the floor. Then one of them charged Astrid, and for a moment she was in real trouble. Melee is not where she lives. She got herself clear just as Garran finished the big one and came over to back her up. They put down every last mannequin without anyone getting too badly hurt, though Ralo caught some damage and needed patching up after.

The locksmiths arrived, changed the locks, and turned out to be the chatty sort. The deed had belonged to the late Lady Sorvellan for a long time, they said. They also told the crew to make friends with the neighbors and go see the local magic shops. Then they got bold and pressed the party to prove they were the rightful owners. Lucien buried them in legalese until they backed down. Once things were friendly again, the locksmiths let slip something better. They had been hired once before to put a magical arcane lock on a secret door up on the third floor. They never learned what was behind it, and they never knew the Lady Sorvellan's password. But with her gone, they were willing to convey the manufacturer's password reset: Ironsmith.

The crew kept exploring. On the mantle they found a pair of strange vials, one holding a thick black tar and the other an inky blue fluid. Ralo found a small flap cut into the wall, like a doggy door for something that is not a dog. Milo found the secret door. They spoke the password and walked into a treasure trove. A safe house, fully stocked. Dungeoneer's packs, explorer's packs, disguise kits, forged papers, 150 gold that Milo mostly pocketed, and a little black book full of scores.

The den belonged to the Silver Serpent, a known vigilante criminal who made a career of hitting noble families. The book held at least half a dozen scores, planned out and never executed. The crew decided to keep it. A book like that is a to-do list if you squint. The flap in the kitchen wall got an answer too. The house has a pet. A baby gelatinous cube lives in the walls and handles the garbage and the scraps. When they woke it up it snatched the prosciutto right out of Ralo's pocket before setting to work cleaning the place.

Then the vials. Lucien uncorked the inky blue one and out came a small ink spirit who speaks only in rhyming couplets. It refused to give its name, since names have power, so Lucien dubbed it Quill Bill. Quill Bill pledged itself to the party as the rightful owners of the house. Quill Bill is an Ink Spirit of Hope, but his brother is a Trickster Spirit of Mischief, and as Quill Bill explained, he's not everyone's cup of tea. They had been rescued from the Tabula Rasa by the Silver Serpent, and have served the house ever since. Quill considers the transcribing and note-taking for the household to be his sacred duty. The black tar vial, presumably the brother, stayed corked.

We leave our heroes poring over the Silver Serpent's black book in their new home when a knock comes at the door. A neighbor named Olivier, carrying a crystal the size of a ball, come to introduce them to something the locals call the Aether-waves.
