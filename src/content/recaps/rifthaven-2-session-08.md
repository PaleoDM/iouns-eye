We opened with the copy finished and ready for pickup. Milo borrowed Astrid's pearl necklace to wear Quarterstaff's face and went to collect it, leaving Thrall behind as collateral. Astrid spent the morning with an undead valet copying her field notes into illuminated calligraphy, and Quill Bill was crestfallen at being passed over for the work.

They came around the corner and found a very fancy carriage outside the shop with FQ on the door. The real Quarterstaff was inside, come about the letters Sylvara had been writing him all fortnight. She wanted to know when she got to wear him on her arm at parties.

Ralo slipped around back into a garden of bamboo grown for wind instruments and a tree being trained into a fiddle curl, and kicked over a trash can. Sylvara blamed the parrots and went out to look, and Milo walked in the front wearing her suitor's face.

Quarterstaff crossed the room faster than a man his age should, took Milo by the lapels and put him against the wall. He wanted to know who this was and whether it was him at the gala. Milo told him he was his best self, the thing he had always wanted to be. The man he was wearing leaned in closer. "I want in. I want a fraction of what you're doing."

Lucien ended it with a subtle, heightened suggestion. Quarterstaff has advantage on saves against being charmed because he is that self-obsessed, and he failed by one anyway. He set Milo down, straightened his own lapels on Milo's body, pinched his own cheek and kissed himself. He had had too much again. This always happens when he has had too much. He confessed that he drinks all night because he dreams of the nightlife, and that someday he hopes to be initiated among the kindred, and told his own best self how nice it was to see him. Then he got in the carriage and went home to bed. It was ten in the morning.

Sylvara came back smitten and a little puzzled that her suitor had stopped playing hard to get. She told Quarterstaff she was glad Bardoof had not come along, because she did not want to see what that man would do with it once he got his hands on it. Milo paid her 25 gold out of his own purse. They left with a lute in a mahogany case, and it was everything she had promised.

They came to House Ondorel at dusk and found three small air skiffs waiting, each one a little flying canoe, each one carrying a case that might hold the Evenbough. Three teams, three routes, and nobody was told which case was real. Ottavia Brann gave Barran a terse salute and thanked his team for answering the call. Garran carried the forgery in his backpack. Astrid took the necklace back and made herself into a nobody, a face assembled out of five commoners from the market.

Their sixth was Hedda Marchbank, a dwarven woman with fiery orange curls braided down both sides of her face, contracted master abjurer and their pilot for the night. She had already put the wards up and did not know or want to know whether the thing under them was genuine. She laid out three danger points. The customs house at the Coronet Gate, where the wards come down for inspection and go back up. The rift crossing, where a skiff cannot hold the open air and has to be towed by gondola. The hangar at the end, where the wards come down again to keep from setting off the alarms. She asked the crew for privacy while she worked, trade secrets, professional courtesy.

Astrid offered her an emergency chinchilla for messages. Hedda took it for a snack, asked whether it was true they taste like chocolate, and then let it climb up onto her shoulder and named it Choco. She declined the parrot. She is terrified of flying, does not care for birds, and intended to spend the whole crossing belayed to the skiff.

Their route was the most direct of the three, which left everyone wondering whether that made them the decoy.

At the gate, Astrid put Pocket up over the district and caught something worth the whole trip. There were messenger birds working back and forth near the gondola stations, and nothing that size flies over the rift by accident. Somebody was coordinating up there. The cost was that their best set of eyes was pointed outward and not at Hedda's hands. Lucien watched the wards go down from across the deck, at distance and at disadvantage, and came away sure of only one thing. He could not do that himself. The wards were down about two minutes. The officials stamped the manifest and waved them through.

The skiff was hooked to a gondola and towed out over the rift, and they were still climbing when the griffins came in. Two riders fast under cover of dark, with more behind them.

Garran opened with a fog cloud and shaped it into an L across the deck so the crew could step in and out of cover. That decided the fight. Nobody could see anybody for the rest of it. The gondola stopped, the operator threw a spell over the cabling, and somebody down the line yelled rift pirates, which is apparently common enough to have a procedure. Hedda spent the battle clutching the rope with Choco in her ear, calming her down and calling out where everyone was.

The riders dismounted into the fog and went for the crate. They could not find it either.

Milo hit one of them with dissonant whispers and the man ran blind, caught the rail at speed and went over the side. He spent his entire next turn climbing back up and dragged himself over the rail by half a hand. Garran stepped out of the cloud once and came face to face with a beautiful parakeet man holding a javelin made of wind, shot him out of his flight, and stepped back in.

A griffin got its claws into Ralo and flew off with him. It had a plan. Halflings in armor are shelled things, and shelled things get dropped on rocks until they open. Astrid cast speak with animals and made it an offer, that she would feed it better than the people who had saddled it that morning, and rolled a 19 to sell it. The griffin turned around.

On the way back Ralo rolled a natural 20 on acrobatics and got into the saddle. He flew it into the last aarakocra and unloaded, and the griffin took the man out of the air, and the two griffins tore him apart between them and threw the pieces into the sky. Ralo stuck the ride through all of it. Not one person saw a second of it.

Astrid shot two more down in starry form, one of them the handler of the griffin she was busy recruiting. Lucien took a rend, put up a shield, and set off a wild magic surge that lit anything nonmagical he touched on fire, aboard a wooden gondola hanging over a rift. Garran ate twenty-six points of greatsword and held the fog cloud through every point of it, then finished his man with a wrathful smite.

When the cloud came down it had rained viscera on all of them. Astrid unsaddled both griffins and let them go, and came away with seventy pounds of tack and nowhere to put it. They owe her one favor, and they will come if she calls.

They took their short rest on the crossing and came up on the far side, high in the cliffs above where griffins fly. Witherwind at dawn on the twentieth, the *Meridian Queen* at dock with her sails furled. The other two skiffs had come in ahead of them, both scorched.

Hedda brought the wards down one last time, and this time Ralo and Garran watched her hands. They found what Lucien could not. Invisible runes are worked into the crate itself at particular spots, and the crew knows where every one of them sits. The magic is still beyond them. The traps are not.

Lord Ondorel inspected it himself, laid out strips of ivory, worked a ritual, and pronounced it real. He remarked on the last memory it gave him, an upsetting one, that he ascribed to the poor first chair from the symphony being a bundle of nerves. He doesn't know it was actually Sylvara who was last to touch it, her emotions a-flutter from her proximity to Quarterstaff at the time.

Lord Ondorel thanked them for bringing in Corvi, and tasked Ottavia with placing the instrument into the vault. Then, complicated things with an invitation. The Queen sails in a few hours, and there is a brunch first. He insists they come as his guests.

We leave our scoundrels at dawn, still wearing the fight, with the real Evenbough going down the other gangplank into the hold on Ottavia's arm. They still hold one thing the Corvis knew about that vault. The window is open, and they are expected at the party.
