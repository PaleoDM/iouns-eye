We opened on the road to the press, our scoundrels still tucked into the baker's cart and turning the job over. The talk was all about the printer. Did they have any leverage to make her sit on the plate? Did they need any? In the end they decided they didn't. They would not lean on her and they would not come back. They would walk in as the distributors, take everything, and disappear. No dead-man's switch, no return trip. Just a lie and a clean exit.

They came up on the press to find they weren't the only ones with an interest. A Tabula agent was already at the door, leaning on the printer, trying to talk or bully his way inside and get the goods handed over. Our crew did not let that go long. Garran and Astrid came down on the man hard, intimidating him half out of his skin, and then Milo put the fear in him proper. Five centuries of vampire will do that. The man broke and ran. He got out one thing on his way, that reinforcements were close behind, and then he was gone into the dark.

That put a clock on it. They knocked. The printer wasn't having any of it, not from a Tabula thug and not from a crew of strangers on her step at that hour. So Lucien went soft. Soothing words, patient ones, enough that she cracked the door. That was all Lucien needed. He slid a Subtle Suggestion through the gap, and his wild magic answered the way it likes to, with a blast of blinding light that filled the doorway. When it cleared the printer could not see a thing. She never got a look at any of their faces. They pushed inside, told her they were the distribution service come for the run, and walked out with every broadsheet and the plate besides. They were gone before the reinforcements ever turned the corner.

Then came the part where you don't get caught holding it. Unsure where else to go and wanting to shake any last Tabula tail, they crashed at Milo's flat and laid low. When it felt safe they moved the contraband to a drophouse Garran knew, an old distillery buried under a shady little temple of Glittergold called the GoldSoul. The man who watches goods down there is a fellow named Zedediah. He took his usual five gold without complaint. Then he pressed Garran for something extra, not coin. He wanted a good word put in with Ralo. Now that Ralo had split from the missus, Zedediah thought the two of them might share a pint and a bite sometime. Garran agreed to pass it along. The contraband got put away safe, and that was that.

With the goods stashed, they went to collect. The Sorvellans came, or rather Vendris did. The elder never showed his face. He sent the boy and a pack of goons in his place. Vendris was rattled. Scared and disappointed both, and genuinely spooked that these gutter scoundrels had pulled off both halves of the job. He had money for half the pay and no more, and he paid it. Our crew made sure he felt the rest of it. They talked loud about distributiong the flyers themselves and putting them out across the city, since the Sorvellans clearly meant to stiff them.

Vendris made a whole show of it. His father was morally bankrupt, he said, and maybe bankrupt in the plain sense too. But he had one thing of his own, a gift from his mother, that could almost cover the other half of the debt. A deed to a shophouse in the Inkwell district. Our scoundrels put their heads together and took the deal. Garran leaned on the boy until he agreed he still owed another hundred gold on top of the deed, and with that the thing was done.

And then it went sideways. Vendris tried to slip a Message spell to someone, quiet, and Astrid caught it and cut it off. They pulled him aside and he came clean. The goons weren't here to escort him. They were here to kill the crew once the coin changed hands. Steel came out.

Vendris took cover and stayed there. Garran walked straight up to the biggest of the goons and let his frightening aura do the work, terror rolling off him. Ralo went off on another of them, unloading everything he had, spending down every last resource just to keep his own feet under him after a brutal hit landed. Milo kept him standing, locking that same goon down with Dissonant Whispers again and again so it never got a clean swing at Ralo. Lucien and Astrid worked the rest, landing blow after devastating blow, and between the five of them the fight didn't last. The goons went down.

Vendris, still breathing thanks to them, agreed to pay off the rest of the debt. He'd spin his own story too, that his father's men died in the fighting while he barely escaped with his life. Our crew, for their part, meant to burn most of the broadsheets and be done with it. The manifesto that started all this ended up ash and leverage, and the job was a clean success.

We leave our heroes some days on, walking up on their new property in Inkwell for the first time. A deed in hand, a debt still owed, and a roof of their own to go look at. Whatever a crew of scoundrels does with a place like that, we'll find out soon enough.
