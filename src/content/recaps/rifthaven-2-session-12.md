We opened back at the den, some time after the whiskey job. Olivier, the crew's halfling neighbor and Aethercaster, showed up with a cracked crystal and played them footage from the Rift cliffs. It showed cranes asleep on their nests, then something huge and gangly crossing the dark behind them, then a scream. Olivier ran when he heard it, and he could not make himself stop. His crystal man, Kalish, slipped behind him and has not been seen since.

Olivier wanted to know what was killing the widow cranes. Their tears go into every striking rune in the city, and the licensed rookery that harvests them had shut down. His show was also about to lose its slot to an infomercial. He could not pay. He came to them because the neighborhood calls the crew a mystery squad, and is very clear that they are not heroes. Olivier could see why. Astrid pointed out that she is technically only morally obligated to deal with poachers, and took the job anyway. Her price was a weekly guest spot where she brings on animals and he holds them. Ralo and Garran asked to be executive producers.

Olivier sang them an old Goliath song about the Widowmaker, learned by ear in a language he does not speak. Astrid had Quill Bill translate it. The tears come from a gland on the beak, the fathers die feeding their chicks, and the hens mourn for five years. The Widowmaker comes when the widows are not allowed to mourn. Astrid went outside and checked the Widow, the constellation the cranes follow. The star at the tip of its beak had gone dim.

At the rookery Astrid announced a welfare inspection. She has a charisma of eight, and she rolled very high on intimidation anyway. Ralo told the foreman they were from the Honeywell District, apiary division, reassigned for budget cuts. Oskra Deepwell let them in, then spotted Olivier and called him a narc. Astrid lifted his crystal before he could protest, and Oskra started talking.

Her harvesters know a dance that lets them get close to a mourning hen, and they never take more than a third of her tears. Even so the colony was shrinking, and some nests had been emptied with no sign of a fight. Milo and Ralo leaned on her with a 24 on persuasion. A month ago she took a sack of coin to let a young Irisour son take a lady he was courting through the nests at night. Astrid told her she had made the stars very angry. Oskra made Sehanine's sign and asked how to fix it.

Oskra had two of her harvesters, Pim and Sefa, winch the crew down the cliff face. On the way they got their first look at a widow crane. They stand as tall as a person and are the color of a gargoyle, with swollen red glands on either side of the beak. Astrid and Milo found it lovely. Everyone else found it terrifying.

Half a mile along the path, three manticores came up from below. The lead one recognized Oskra and told her sisters that the little hag had brought them fresh prey. Ralo took comfort that at least they could not shoot anything, and then she swung her tail around and showed him a quiver of spikes.

Garran and Astrid shot the same manticore, and between them they had two dead by the second round. Lucien caught two in a Slow. Then a chimera came roaring over the clifftop and breathed fire on Milo, Astrid and Lucien. Ralo got into it with whip and scimitar, and every cut slowed it down. Milo hit it with dissonant whispers, and it fled forty feet up the rocks through attacks from three of them. Garran cast Jump, went up the cliff after it, and ran it through. Then he had to jump back down. Astrid finished the last manticore, and Olivier came running up to heal Lucien.

Up ahead, a fourth manticore had gotten into Pim and Sefa. They had fought it off, but they were full of spikes. Oskra offered to winch everyone back up and call the temple. The crew asked if they could just sit down for a bit instead. Garran helped carry the wounded to the first nesting plateau, about fifteen nests built out of scavenged boulders.

Astrid spent the short rest redoing her hair to match the cranes' frills and ritual casting speak with animals. The nearest hen told her to keep her distance. Pocket vouched for her. "Yes, she's my pet. Domestic human." The hen said it was good to see some of them learning their place.

The hen told Astrid the Widow was dimming, and the hens had been talking. Nests with chicks had gone empty, with no feathers and no blood, and widows were not being allowed to mourn. She would not say who was doing it. Astrid was still a human, and humans might be trying to trick them.

Astrid could not do the dance, and Pim and Sefa were in no shape to. So Milo did it, with the two of them coaching from the ground and Astrid translating. He rolled a 37. Pim spent eight years at bard school and took it badly. The hen fanned herself with a wing and asked whether Milo was spoken for. She would tell Astrid everything, on the condition that Milo visits once a fortnight and learns to recite something dreadfully melancholy.

The rest came out of the hen in pieces, passed along from nest to nest. A month ago a young man in fancy plumage and the lady he was courting led strangers to the cliffs. The strangers carried glass bottles of something they called ink, in reds and blues and purples, and they took tears in them. Since then the strangers come on their own, on a loud thing whose wings spin in circles. They carry sticks of fire, and they take chicks soon after the fathers die. They were seen yesterday at the bigger nesting sites. The flock is split on whether to follow the Widow this season, and some have already gone. Astrid taught the hen a new word for them, poachers.

Astrid is only morally obligated to deal with poachers, and now she had some. She also thought she knew who they were. On the crew's first job, a new gang called Tabula Rasa had been chasing a woman over ink magic, and the crew decided it was not their problem. Astrid is fairly sure it is their problem now.

Oskra asked whether she needed to come along, because she would love to get the spikes out of her people. Astrid told her the Irisour boy was in bed with a very nasty gang, and luckily she was morally obligated to kill them. Oskra put a hand on her shoulder. "Besides my grandma and my mom, you're the baddest bitch I know." She cast Shield of Faith on Astrid and took her wounded back up the cliff, to untangle her ledger from a noble boy.

On the way across the plateau Milo, drunk on the attention, petted a crane on the far side. It was the wrong one. Shae frilled up at him, the first hen told her to back off because she had claimed him, and Pocket told the ladies this one was still being domesticated. The crew left them fighting.

The cranes pointed them up a path to a cave they call a place of predators. Milo carried the torch. There were bats everywhere, which he found comforting. Two hundred feet in, his boot crunched on a long bone. Something fifteen feet long hung from the ceiling, wrapped in black wings. It opened them like a manta ray, showed two red eyes, and shrieked.
