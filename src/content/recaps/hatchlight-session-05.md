We opened on the 5th of Pelor, a few days south of Glancoed. Our heroes were on the road to Dr. Riddenour's estate past Aberblaidd, and for once it was not raining.

The lights had been at work on all of them. Harsk was in a better mood. The cold did not bother him anymore, and some nights he forgot anyone else needed a fire. Rasa wore a new face most days, a dwarf one morning and pink hair the next, with a glow under the skin. Around strangers he was still Laeryn. Bringha was less clumsy, and she paid attention to the wind now. Turquoise lines ran up her forearm. Yu had gotten a hat in the mail and cut holes in it for his horns, not neatly. He had grown up a little. Indri had built Slate a new body out of painted bones. He ran on personality and not much memory, and he talked to her inside her head.

The road south ran through the neck of the peninsula, and Culdir was the one real stop on it. It was also famous for a battle. The giants had marched an army of pressed small folk through the neck, and two titans set their squabbles aside to stop it, just this once. Harsk's people called the storm titan Voshgaroth, the one who goes at the vanguard. Indri grew up calling the cold titan Morosko. The giants' army still stood frozen in the north field, and the neck had been bitter cold ever since. All five brands pulled them toward it.

They arrived around one in the afternoon, in the middle of a midwinter festival. Children fought with wooden swords. Plenty of people sold food. Nobody sold sweets.

Bringha went straight for something fried. An old woman had lizards on a spit, which were easy to catch in winter because you just flipped them over. She told Bringha that Mrs. Edith was the best pastry baker in the whole damn world, and her kids were grown, so she could say that. The recruiters had been rounding up the good rations, and a few young men were lined up across the green. Edith had won the pie contest fifty-four years running. A baker named Fill thought he could take her, but a meat pie was not winning in midwinter. Bringha said sometimes you need to warm your bones. The woman's face went blank, like she had never considered it.

Rasa went people watching. He had always studied people to work out what he was missing. An apple seller, dunking the last of the season in spiced butter for lack of caramel, told him Fill was Culdir's great comeback story. He had shown up a ruffian, an adventurer put him in his place, and now he baked. The man said he and Fill went back a little, and Rasa could tell he was underselling it. Nobody down here had sugar, he said. Edith always seemed to have a little left, the way some people kept whiskey in the cellar.

Most people gave Indri's brightly painted bone gargoyle a wide berth. A girl of maybe twelve asked how much. Slate was not for sale, and the girl did not buy it. "This is the second cool little lizard with wings that totally isn't a dragon thing I've seen today." Indri said the other one was not hers. The girl was Fill's apprentice, and she decided Indri was probably a spy. She told her to keep her merchandise away from her master's table until after the judging. Indri said she was merely existing in this space. The girl said she was watching her. Indri asked Bringha to tie the girl's shoelaces together.

The bell rang and everyone got a slice of each pie. Fill's was a meat pie, flaky like a strudel. Edith's was a tray of berry tarts, and none of those berries were in season. Bringha watched the crowd eat, saw nothing wrong, and stuck to the meat pie anyway. Harsk ate both, because a woodsman never turns down a meal offered in good faith. So did Rasa.

Edith, a sweet little halfling woman, took her fifty-fifth win. About five percent of the town voted for Fill. His apprentice went to comfort him and fell face first into a pie. Then she told him Edith was cheating, and did not even have an oven in her shop. Fill clapped a hand over her mouth. It had never been about winning, he told Chippy, and one of those adventurers was a very special man who had saved his life. Someday she would inherit the shop, Edith would be dead, and she could win every year. Chippy made a rude gesture and stormed off. Rasa told Fill it was a damn good pie, and Fill clapped him on the back.

Fill was packing up when Edith screamed for help. Her shop was burning a block away, and she was trapped inside. Fill shouted for a bucket brigade, and the town ran for the spring. The fire was drawing spirits, delighted to find a live one. One stood in Edith's doorway, man-shaped, with black flaming skin and eyes like magma. It told Bringha, in Primordial, to step back, because this one was its to consume. A salamander told Rasa they had been waiting. The wood was so dry. Always things in the oven, and never a fire lit.

Bringha got there first. She chilled her fists and sprayed cold water across one corner of the house, and that corner stopped burning. Harsk put an arrow in the salamander, and it told him a child of cold did not belong here. Harsk laughed at it, and Rasa finished it off. A magmin set itself on fire, hugged the wall, and taunted Rasa.

The big one broke the door down and put Bringha on the ground. Edith shouted through the window to ask Rasa whether to run for it. He had just watched Bringha drop, and he told her to go. She went straight through the fire and out.

Indri rubbed up some static and sent it into Bringha. Bringha came back up, and the lightning arced into everything around her and killed the other salamander. Rasa brought his maul down on the big one's head. Before his smite could take, the earth rumbled, pebbles rose, and it went out like a fire with no air. He laid hands on Bringha on his way past. Bringha punched Indri's lightning into the magmin, and it exploded all over her and Rasa. Yu's guiding bolt popped the second magmin, and Indri was short enough to duck.

Edith threw herself into Harsk's arms and told him he was so brave. The town had the fire out within half an hour, and the shop was saved. Edith told everyone she must have run her stove too hot. The shop had no oven and no chimney. Privately, she asked the five of them to find out who did it. Yu could tell she did not know, and that she was playing it down so the town would not turn on Fill. Harsk read her fear. Fill had been a changed man since that Laeryn set him straight, she said, but his baking had been getting better, suspiciously so. She was not afraid of dying in a fire. She was afraid of losing next year.

Indri painted her eyelids and cast detect magic. Everyone who ate Edith's tarts carried a fading trace of enchantment. Bringha had none. It looked like charmed pie and nothing worse. Edith's cane was magic too. Rasa used his brand to see what kept her fire lit, and took the question literally. The illusion on her cane slipped, and for a moment it was a candy cane.

Inside, someone had pushed a cart against the front door and stacked crates under the shutters. Someone had tried to kill her. The burn spread in a cone from one corner, projected from inside the house. By the door lay one of Fill's meat pies, half-eaten and scorched. Over the mantel hung a faintly magic portrait of a mother, a father and five-year-old twins. Bringha recognized one of the twins as Edith.

It took Yu a while to notice the man drinking under a tree. "Well, it took you long enough." He had seen the whole thing, and he liked Yu's hat. Everyone thought it was Fill, he said, but it was a lizard, a big yellow one about the size of a goat. He wanted a bottle of whiskey for the rest, and Yu paid him five silver instead. Chippy had come first, found the door locked, and stormed off back toward Fill's. Then the lizard lit the place up and flew off northeast, toward the woods. He knew lizards do not fly. It flew anyway. He started to introduce himself and passed out halfway through. His name was Peetra Sylver, and that is where we leave our heroes.
