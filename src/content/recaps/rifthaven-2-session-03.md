We opened the moment the shape dropped. The thing wearing Tomis came on with the shillelagh, and the two strangers it had brought turned out to be no strangers at all. They were spiders. Araneas. The fight was on.

It got rough fast. Ralo ended up alone with both araneas, and for a stretch it looked bad. He spent three full rounds taking the pair of them on by himself, surviving on nothing but footwork and nerve, dodging and weaving until the rest of the crew could fight their way across to him. Garran planted himself in the doorway and held it, keeping the false Tomis from ever getting properly into the room. Milo caught the thing with a Dissonant Whispers and sent it fleeing, burning a whole turn just to come back. Lucien and Astrid worked the back line, and their fire is what turned it. When the crew finally converged on Ralo, the spiders went down quick after that.

When it was done, Lucien set to burning. As much as he could, as fast as he could, to put the scene behind them. But they needed a breath first, so they took a short rest, and while they did they went over the bodies. That is where they found it for the first time. A seal. Blank. Not a single mark pressed into it. Whoever these people answered to, it was not anyone they had met yet. Someone else was in the game.

They finished the rest and had Wrenna take them to Maggie's safe house, quick as she could manage. They were followed the whole way, and they knew it. There was no other road to walk. They carried themselves hard enough that nobody moved on them, and they came up on the Almshouse in one piece.

It is a rough place. People sleeping outside, a line waiting on bread. But the folk here protect it, and the rent collectors trailing the crew would not come near. They were not welcome in that space and they knew it. Inside, the matron of the Almshouse looked the crew over with no warmth at all. She made them work for it. They had to be convincing. In the end it was Wrenna's word that carried, and the matron relented, poured them some wine, and let them down to see Maggie.

The conversation with Maggie was touch and go. She was wounded, and her room told the rest of the story. Conspiracy writing covered every surface, scrawled and stacked, the work of a woman trying to get as much of herself down on paper as she could before it was gone. She knew her position was thin. She also knew our crew, and our crew are not the kind to lead with what they want. So there was a dance. Maggie working out what they were after, working out what she had left to trade, all of it complicated by the fact that she plainly owed them for saving Wrenna.

When the ask finally came, they got their answers. The manifesto was here. Maggie had it. She had a soft spot for the younger Sorvellan and open contempt for the elder, for what his name does to the people of Gutterwick. And she was being hunted by the Tabula Rasa, an outfit chasing a device the table came to call the Mimeofoil. A machine that forges any hand you feed it. To calibrate it they need a real one, sealed and genuine, and the manifesto written in Vendris's own hand is exactly that. Maggie tried to appeal to their better natures. A world where that machine runs is a world where no signature, no deed, no printed word can be trusted, and the whole order rots from there. But our heroes are not heroes. They are scoundrels, and the end of trust as a concept did not move them much.

What moved them was the catch. Maggie had the manifesto, but its linocut had already gone to the printer. The words were already on their way to the press. Stopping that was the back half of the job, which meant only half their payday was sitting in this room. The rest of it was locked behind a printing press somewhere across the city.

Then Astrid had a thought, with Milo helping her reason it through. The ink leaking out of Maggie's wound, the same unwriting they had seen take Sunflower, might answer to the seal they had pulled off the doppelganger. She offered to try. In exchange, the manifesto and the location of the press. Maggie did not have much room to refuse, and after some good rolls she let Astrid work. It held. The healing took. Maggie came out of it a great deal chipper than she went in, and she made good. She gave up the printer's location and handed over the manifesto.

We leave our heroes slipping out under cover of night, tucked into a baker's cart, rolling for the printing press. Half a job done, half still waiting, and the hope that they reach the press before the Tabula Rasa does.
