We opened two hundred feet into the cave the cranes called a place of predators, with Milo holding the only torch. The thing unfolding from the ceiling was a cloaker. It had no eyes, a mouth full of grinding teeth, and a leather harness strapped across its back. Two phase spiders blinked in from somewhere else to join it.

Ralo went at it with whip and scimitar and could not land a thing. He pointed out that they were not even getting paid for this. Astrid shot it with a starlight arrow and then her musket, and Lucien set it on fire.

The cloaker moaned, and Astrid and Ralo were too frightened to get near it. Then it snuffed Milo's torch, and the cave went black. It dropped onto Ralo and wrapped him up whole. Garran had to ask whether Astrid's starry form gave off any light. It did, and it was all they had.

Garran attacked it anyway, knowing every blow would land on Ralo as much as the cloaker. Ralo understood. He asked to be resurrected in something taller. Pocket flew in to help, Milo called out some encouragement, and Ralo shoved his way out of the thing.

Lucien caught all three monsters in a Slow. His magic surged and left him healing on his own for a while, as if he had just remembered a compliment from fifteen years ago. Astrid put up an aura of healing light. The slowed cloaker took one swing at Garran and missed, and Garran ran it through the throat. His rapier caught on a hook at the back of its collar.

Milo's dissonant whispers sent a spider running past Garran and Ralo, and Ralo cut it on the way by. Astrid shot that one dead, and Garran carved up the last one like Zorro. Astrid's aura was still going when the fighting stopped, and she used it to patch everyone up.

The harness had a big iron ring at the back, the kind you run a chain through. The cloaker was somebody's guard dog. Astrid said that if her cryptid turned out to be a fake, she was going to be pissed.

It had been guarding a crossroads, three tunnels and a lot of bones. As Astrid's starlight swept the walls, Garran caught scratchings that reminded him of thieves' cant. Astrid read them as Druidic. They were a protocol for getting past the cloaker, either which prey to bring or which song to sing. The tunnels were marked dead end, way out, and home. They took home.

The tunnel ran three hundred yards and opened on a cramped cavern that looked like a thieves' den, with bedrolls and a row of cages. Kalish was huddled in one, squinting away from Astrid's light.

Most of the room was taken up by something twice the size of the cloaker. It had slate-gray hide, obsidian plates down its back, and the long beak and crest of a pterodactyl. It tried to bellow at the light, but an iron muzzle held its beak shut. Its wings were manacled to the floor, and a chain held its neck down. Where its glands should have been, burst sacs had crusted into garnet crystals.

It lunged at Astrid against the chains. Olivier said it was muzzled for a reason, and skirted the room to get to Kalish.

Astrid cased the place with Ralo's help. There were prods, whips, alchemy bottles, and a loop on a pole for pinning a head down. Someone had cut the glands open and drained them. Astrid recognized the hand in the Druidic on the walls. It belonged to the Circle of the Broken Tusk, fringe druids most of Rifthaven called terrorists. Astrid decided they were hardly druids at all, which made them poachers, and poachers had to die.

Milo studied it and recognized something from his old life. It had been changed the way his cousins of the night were changed. Astrid thought of grasshoppers turning into locusts. This had been a widow crane who was not allowed to mourn, and the other cranes no longer called her kin.

Milo did the crane dance and finished in a full split, and the thrashing eased. Astrid ritual cast speak with animals while he kept dancing. It came up in time to hear Pocket assuring the creature that Astrid was almost done, because it took her a while to relearn.

"I know I had a real name once, but you might as well call me Livid."

Astrid asked how she felt about killing poachers, and Livid said she would kill anything she could reach with her beak. Then she asked whether they had her chicks.

Astrid remembered the verse of the Goliath song about the cranes following the Widow. She took her starry form and cast the Widow across the ceiling, bright and whole, the way it had looked before it dimmed. Livid calmed. The hatred felt like a chain around her heart, she said, worse than the ones on her wings.

Astrid asked Milo for something sad, and he gave Livid a couplet.

"The bottle has run dry, yet I can't summon tears to cry."

Livid said she would enjoy working out what the bottle meant. The puzzle kept the rage off her.

She asked to rest before she joined them, and whether that was why they took eggs, to poach them. Astrid explained that it was less of a cooking thing, and unshackled her. Livid gave them directions to the poachers' nest, three quarries over and down a hidden ravine. Then she gave Astrid the last two drops from her glands.

It was Widow's Bane, far more volatile than tears. That was the real business. They made these monsters on purpose to bleed them. Astrid made sure Livid understood that griffins were friends, not food. That was a hard one for her. Ralo took a whip with a nail through the end.

Olivier had Kalish back, which technically finished the job. He wanted to come anyway, because he could heal and he felt responsible, but he did not want to kill anyone. He was also still not paying. He asked for his crystal back so he could film the poachers, and Astrid refused to leave any record of what she was about to do. Olivier pitched it as investigative journalism, the Broken Tusk tied to the Irisours, with masks for uniforms. Then he agreed to hang back sixty feet and heal.

They came out of the cave to find an air skiff parked at the cliff edge, the loud thing with spinning wings the cranes had described. Five poachers were climbing off it, a dragonborn, a lizardfolk, an aarakocra, and one who was on fire. The most human of them said the crew had come right to them.

Lucien set the druid on fire. The aarakocra blasted Garran back with a gust of wind, and Garran shot it until the wind stopped. Ralo went at the druid with his whip until it could barely stand. Then Milo cast Hypnotic Pattern. The aarakocra tried to stop it and failed, and all five froze, staring into a shimmer of blood mist.

Nobody wanted to spend more on poachers who could not move. Lucien sent the crane's call echoing into the cave instead. Livid came out running on all fours, on wings she could not yet fly with, and the last of Astrid's spell caught her saying she still remembered. She started with the aarakocra and worked through all five. The one on fire did not slow her down. The noises unsettled most of the party. Lucien figured that was what poachers got.

She sat back on her haunches, satisfied, and looked northwest toward her chicks. They mimed sleep at her, and she went back into the cave to rest.

Judging by the den and the skiff, there were a dozen or more poachers left. Nobody knew how fast another widow would turn. The crew had directions, an empty skiff, and a choice to make about whether to sleep first. We leave our scoundrels there for now.
