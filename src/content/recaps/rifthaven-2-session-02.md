We opened the same night we left off. No rest, no morning. The crew walked straight out of the Brimstone Bath into the evening and made for the Halworth Reading Rooms.

The Halworth is the kind of quiet that costs money. A gnome named Sunflower met them at the door, a cat winding around her ankles. The whole place was wrong for this crew. Garran stuck out most of all. 

They spread out and started searching.

Astrid went for the cat. A ritual for Speak with Animals, a bribe of catnip, and the cat decided she was worth its time. It led her up to the third floor and told her what it knew. There was an attic above, behind a hidden door, and the door wanted a password.

Downstairs the rest worked Sunflower. She told them others had already come around asking after La Marginalia. When they pressed for a description she came apart. A man. A woman. Elvish, maybe. Dwarven, maybe. She could not hold the answer still. Then her nose started to bleed. Milo, five centuries a vampire, knows blood when he sees it, and that was not blood. It was ink. Her memory of whoever had come through had been written over, and the ink was leaking out of the hole they left behind. Ralo found the mercy in it. "Better to have her spacey than scared."

Astrid brought the others up. They found the trapdoor, worked out the password, and let themselves in. They also found 4 books that had their ink sunked completely dry off the pages. Odd. 

The attic held a society of dead scholars, and the meeting was a strange, talky affair. The cabal heard them out and named a price. The party would clear out the poltergeists that had gotten loose upstairs, and in return the ghosts would give up a lead on La Marginalia, who was a close friend. The thread ran through her sister.

So they took the fight. Milo and Astrid set up in the back and threw spells into the middle of it. Lucien landed a clean Hideous Laughter and took out on of the combatants for two rounds. Garran and Ralo took the front and held it like men who have done this before. Everyone caught a little damage. Nobody came close to going down. It did not take long.

With the haunt cleared, Lady Hypatra made good on the deal. She pointed them to Maggie's sister, a woman named Wrenna, down in the Gutterwick district.

Gutterwick after dark is not a place you walk into careless, and they didn't. They came up on Wrenna's door slow. She was inside, drinking. When the knock came she called out, thinking it was her husband. When it wasn't, she went straight to angry and suspicious and kept the door shut.

Lucien talked to her through the crack. He charmed her, and somewhere in it she got the idea they'd come to tell her that her husband was dead. Then his wild magic surged and lit him up head to toe, a blinding white light, and Lucien did not waste it. He played the angel. Wrenna believed.

She talked. Her husband Tomis had always told her to keep her distance from Maggie. He didn't want the trouble. But lately he'd flipped. Now he was obsessed with her. He kept saying the rent collectors had taken an interest, that they'd forgive a whole year of rent if Wrenna gave her sister up. Maggie had been wounded two weeks back and gone to ground in a safe house, and Wrenna would not give her up. She and Tomis had fought about it again and again. She'd held the line.

In the end she agreed to take the crew to the safe house. In part because she'd also mentioned that she knew Maggie planned to move some contraband this very night.

And that is when Tomis came home. He took one look at the scoundrels in his house and his wife halfway out the door, and he didn't ask questions. He told them they were all going to Maggie's right now, and they were going to turn her in, and he raised a shillelagh to make the point. More to the point: they saw his face morphing in real time, rearranging itself into the face of Tomis as the voice pitched down to the match. Ralo instinctively suspected a trick.

We leave our heroes there. A drunk woman, a hidden sister, and a husband who used to be a good man standing in the doorway with a club in his hand. Whatever Tomis is now, he isn't done talking.
