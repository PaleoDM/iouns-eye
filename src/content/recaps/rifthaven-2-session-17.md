We opened a couple of weeks after Gemheart, on the 3rd of Moradin, at the shophouse in Inkwell. Ralo's take from the election was Jelfi's shadow scimitar, a gift from Mammon. It is called the Ace of Blades. When Ralo misses with it, he can go double or nothing. If he loses that too, the house gets a favor to call in later, and it can call it in on any of them.

Then a halfling came calling. He had dark curly hair, an ascot and tasteful jewelry, and he wore his money like someone who has always had it. His name was Pellam Quire, and he spoke for a benefactor he would only call Mr. H. The crew pointed him at Garran, and Garran wanted to know what it paid. It paid five hundred gold apiece, and a thousand more apiece for doing it well. Mr. H keeps one of the most interesting private enchanters in Rifthaven, so wondrous items were on the table. He has no love for the Tabula Rasa, and he had noticed the crew getting in their way.

Mr. H is a financier, and the Order of the Ju-Shi is investigating him. Officially, Mr. H maintains that no crime has been committed. The Order may decide that souls have been trafficked between financial institutions and extraplanar entities. Mr. H would like them to decide it about Severin Mortmain, an old elf and senior Coinwright. Mortmain is under a loose house arrest, and Pellam says he foolishly believes himself innocent. The Order reports on the 10th. Two or three pieces of evidence would make the frame stick, and all six would pay best.

It starts with Mortmain's signet ring. A House seal only counts if the ring itself pressed it, and Mr. H had a replica made for the swap. Mortmain wears the ring every waking hour, and he is warded against charm. At night he gives it to his special pet. Mr. H never asked what kind.

Astrid offered to teach Pellam bear. She has heard it is one of the easiest languages to pick up. He said he found bears rather slothful. When nobody needed anything else from him, Astrid turned into a black bear and chased him out of the house. He left his documents behind.

Sleep was off the table, since Mortmain is an elf. But Astrid sells messenger toucans, and Mortmain is one of her customers. Every bird she sells comes to a call only she knows. They staked out the estate from the back of a borrowed waste management wagon, and Astrid called down a toucan and cast beast sense on it.

Through its eyes she saw caterers arriving at the manor. Mortmain was bored under house arrest and hosting a dinner. The toucan flew home to the stables, where a half-orc stablemaster cut up scrap fruit for it and told it there was no mail today. Behind him, asleep in the hay, was a warthog the size of an elephant. Part of it was machine. It had a steel plate on one shoulder, an antenna off the back of its head, and a light that kept scanning in its sleep. Astrid could not tell whether her beast magic would work on it.

Rather than sabotage the real caterers, the crew called and sold themselves as an add-on. They were wine merchants, an upscale package on top of the catering, with Milo as the sommelier. The caterers turned out to be entirely legitimate. One of their servers was not. She had close-cut black hair with a little purple in it, and she slipped in and out of shadows. She was a monk of the Order of the Ju-Shi, doing her own reconnaissance.

The drinking had started at one. Mortmain turned out to be an agreeable fellow. He assumed a friend had added the wine on, and told them they shouldn't have. He had sent his family abroad to keep them clear of the investigation, and he was bored enough to chat with the help. Astrid came as Lars, the stable boy driving the cart. Bertie Gristle, the stablemaster, watched her work until he was satisfied she knew horses.

Ralo was a charcutier under a vow of silence, and spoke only through his cuts. Milo talked enough for both of them. Ralo handed Mortmain a bell pepper carved into a tulip, and Mortmain said he had fast hands. The signet was on his right hand, with a falcon on it.

A neighbor named Dahlia slid her arm into his and asked for less bite in the wine. They were keeping things light, she said, and nobody wanted Severin in one of his moods. Mortmain told her everyone around him is shady, and he has never claimed to be a saint. But he had no idea what the investigation was about. "It's dark stuff, Dahlia." Milo tried to keep him at the table by talking up the spice notes.

Garran was the other driver, and he waited outside with a cigarette. The idea was to leave the pet nowhere to sleep. When nobody was watching, he snapped his fingers and gave a little bird call. A summoned beast came down in the shape of one of Astrid's toucans, and he handed it the cigarette. Toucans are the pigeons of Rifthaven, and nobody looks twice at one. It dropped the ashes into the hayloft, and the stables went up.

Bertie threw open the barn doors, and Astrid kept up the act and got the horses out. Behind them came four or five basset hounds made of candle wax, and they were terrified of the fire. They all ran straight to Astrid, and one flopped over. Then Bertie led out the warthog. It stood twelve or fifteen feet at the shoulder. Bertie told Tusk it was sleeping in the manor tonight anyway, the way it always did.

When Bertie's back was turned, Astrid cast animal friendship on Tusk, and it took. Then something else in its head pushed back at her. She had felt that once as a girl, speaking with a bumblebee. There were two things in that head. She shut it out. Tusk's steel chest plate had a hinged door in it, like a small safe. Astrid sent all of it to the crew by message.

Up at the house, Mortmain told everyone that Bertie would handle it. Two of his hired hands knew a little magic, and they called up wind and rain over the stables. Then Tusk came barreling around the house, and the manservant opened the French doors. Most guests backed away. A stocky dwarf made a show of scratching it on the way in. Its spot was a huge rug in front of the unlit fireplace.

Lucien slipped away to find the study. The desk was the kind of clean that meant anything important got put away every night. One book on the shelf was really a box made to look like a book, and it was the only one without dust on it. Lucien's detect magic showed a thin silver wire across it. Anyone who lifted the box without the ring would set off an alarm in Mortmain's head. Mortmain reset it every night. Lucien could have dispelled it, but then it would stay dispelled, and Mortmain would know someone had been there. He left it alone.

The bottom drawer of the desk was locked, and something inside it was magic. Lucien picked the lock with thieves' tools. He had learned at boarding school, stealing candy from the other students. Inside was a satchel of four potions. Two were red, one was gold, and one looked like liquid mercury. He took the quicksilver one and locked the rest back up.

Astrid always carried a sedative strong enough for a large animal, in case one needed her help. She had brewed this one with a long fuse. With the stables burning, somebody had to get up on the manor roof and wet it down, and that somebody was the stable boy. Astrid hung the dose inside the chimney on a leather thong, out of reach of any passing squirrel. Pocket could cut it loose whenever they needed it.

Nobody had gone for the ring yet. They figured Mortmain would not hand it off until his guests had gone. Tusk was charmed for a day, the draught was in the chimney, and the box only opened for the ring. They had most of a week, and the monk was still carrying trays. Somebody pitched the wine and charcuterie bill as a motive. We leave our scoundrels there, with dinner coming and an invoice to write.
