We opened with the books closing on the Songbird job. Lord Ondorel's gold cleared, fifteen hundred apiece, and Milo walked out with the groom's blade, forged by a famous Farrowfield artificer. The whole party dinged level five in the same breath.

Then the DM closed last session's cliffhanger. Stella Gatito had left Ralo poisoned in his own chambers, and when she vanished, all that was left behind was a heap of her clothes. The party's gelatinous cube found him first, sucking the venom out before anyone else noticed anything was wrong. Stella's message was blunt: the crew had stood her up twice now, and from the Gatitos' side that reads as sabotage of their peace with the da Pincis. Ralo kept most of it to himself. Deception check or not, the table could tell he was hiding something. Nobody pressed.

A little over a week later, into the month of Glittergold, Garran got the summons instead of Ralo, who'd stood Zedediah up for a promised night out. Zedediah's family whiskey, sealed off two centuries ago when the rift swallowed their ancestral estate, had just been exposed by a fresh tremor. The catch: it sat past a customs line and Griffin-riding wardens, unlicensed and entirely illegal to reach. Zed's father offered church services in trade, a resurrection or a greater restoration on request. Zed brought along Churro, a whistle-whisker with the nose to tell real barrels from decoys, and warned upfront that he had zero combat capability of his own.

The church's air skiff was barely functional and obviously stolen, and Zed's idea of piloting was flying it straight off the edge of the rift into freefall. Along the way he learned that he had spent seventeen years assuming Ralo was a dwarven woman. Griffin-mounted customs wardens spotted the fall and closed in fast. The skiff was undershooting its landing badly enough that Ralo began frantically calling out for Garran to drive and the casters to act. Astrid whistled up a gust of wind to catch the sail and correct their course, saving what would have been a rough landing. When the lead warden set down to question them, Lucien talked their way clear with a straight-faced story about a noble out for a dangerous joyride, capped by a natural twenty. The marshal apologized and rode off.

They landed and found the cave mouth already guarded: an enormous cockatrice, equal parts chicken and bat and ugly at both, with two giant vultures circling down from above.

Combat opened fast. Lucien and Ralo dropped one vulture outright: Lucien with a powerful firebolt and Ralo with a crazy stunt involving a leaping scimitar-and-whip kill in midair. Then he stuck the landing with a Boots of the Winding Path rewind that snapped him straight back to the ledge like he'd been spliced out of a bad movie. Milo and Astrid chipped away at what was left, Astrid's new musket earning its keep. The cockatrice went straight for Ralo, clawing him down to half health, then turned and landed a crit on Garran hard enough to worry the table. Garran didn't fold. Level five's power spike showed up in full: two brutal hits that left the cockatrice looking rough, Hunter's Mark and frightened stacking against it from Lucien's end. Lucien wrapped its legs with a whip, and Ralo and Garran tore in together to finish it.

A short rest, then the actual dungeon crawl started, a first for this party. Ralo took point, banking on his boots to bail him out of trouble. The party's stealth held except for Astrid, who rolled a natural one and got thoroughly distracted by some mysterious animal droppings. Deeper in, Ralo caught a whiff of stale air down a side tunnel and steered Zed away from it, placing the smell as gas from some shady old job. The tunnels opened into the ruins of a real dwarven hall, facing a pair of massive stone doors carved with six faces.

The door read, in dwarven runes only Garran could make out, that homage was owed to each of the six original gods in turn. Zed placed three of the faces right away: Moradin, Glittergold, and Yondola. The party worked out the rest by elimination and the calendar, landing on Ioun the Knowing Mistress, Erathis the Lawbearer, and Bahamut the Platinum Dragon.

Milo went first, producing a mini bottle of sparkling wine and passing it around for a toast to Yondola. The shared drink unlocked the first bolt and left the whole party with advantage on charisma checks against each other for the hour. Astrid offered her illuminated field notes to Ioun and got pulled bodily into a vision of an impossibly vast library, cataloguing her own work. She walked away with one free question saved for later, a nosebleed, and disadvantage on her next five rolls. Zed gave up his family's lucky platinum coin to Glittergold, hoping some future, properly licensed treasure hunter would find it. Ralo, the only one with any relevant proficiency, forged Moradin's offering: a flattering, gender-bent self-portrait that left Zed genuinely moved. Lucien whispered Erathis his favorite law, an ancient and mostly-forgotten ban on long knives in Salvation. Last came Bahamut: a toast to the dwarves lost in the exodus, and a quick, gleeful bit of vandalism on a sketch of Tiamat for good measure.

The sixth lock gave way, and the stone doors opened.

Beyond the doors lay a fully intact stretch of underground dwarven city: a road wide enough for wagons running three abreast into the dark, ceilings lost somewhere overhead, the first real look at what the rift had swallowed two centuries ago. That's where the session ended.
