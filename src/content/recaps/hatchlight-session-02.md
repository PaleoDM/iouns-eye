We opened at the spring, with a promise to keep.

The children came down to the shrine of the Afanc and cleaned the place up properly. Indri led the lesson on the water cycle and did a genuinely great job of it. Bringha delivered the majesty of water through a long series of interpretive dances. What we did not expect was that Laeryn and Yu would both get so invested. They sat in on a lesson meant for children and came away having actually learned things they did not know, thoroughly impressed by Indri's grasp of the material and by how she delivered it.

Partway through, they caught something at the treeline. A strange crystalline light, bluish, sitting right at the forest's edge. It felt cold. Not unwelcoming, though. Just cold. Nobody went to look.

The children scattered and Gwenllian came down with Eirlys's notes from her hut. The party put the pieces together. The poisoning of the Gloaming, the sickening, had been going on a long time, and that part was not news. What was new was that people had started going missing, and that the old alder deep in the wood had been getting sicker as it happened. Eirlys had made up her poultices and her potions and gone in after both problems at once. She meant to heal the alder, and she meant to find Rhydderch, the trapper who had not come back.

Our heroes decided to go in after her. They had firsthand accounts from the handful of people who had been inside the Gloaming and come out again, and they had Harsk. Harsk rolled very well on knowing where the paths in there run, and we decided in the fiction that he had spent a night at Rhydderch's camp himself at some point, the two of them overlapping on the road and sharing a fire. That bought him real inside knowledge. The catch was the other half of it. They could follow the trail confidently for as long as they believed Eirlys had stayed on a path. The moment she left one, it was going to get very hard to keep her.

The Gloaming got worse the deeper they went. Fewer birds. Fewer signs of anything living at all. A few hours in, they walked into a clearing full of ancient giant technology, scattered and half-sunk into the ground, with a body poking out from underneath one of the pieces.

They spread out to investigate and the clearing came apart on them. The dead here had been raised by something that was part necromancy and part Second Age arcanotech, giant-made and somehow still running. The things carried a fear aura, and there were a few moments where it looked like somebody was going to break. It went their way anyway. For the second session running the enemies turned out to be weak to fire, and Indri's fire bolt kept coming up maximum damage. Laeryn finally got his legs under him. He put down the hammer he had fumbled with in the first session and went in with a short sword and scimitar together, and it made all the difference.

The body under the machine was another trapper. A drover, one of the nomad folk off the steppe. It looked like they had climbed up inside the husk to take shelter and never come out, and they were still clutching something. Laeryn tried to pull it free with a mage hand and it was simply too heavy, so Indri and the others climbed up into the wreck and got it out by hand. A crystalline bead. A bead of nourishment, which makes clean water, and in the Gloaming there is not much worth more than that. The water and the air here are both poisoned.

There was a snake in with them, small, and it did not stay. Yu cast detect poison and found the corpse riddled with venom, which made the snake the obvious culprit. Harsk started a speak with animals ritual to ask it about that. By the time the ritual finished, the snake was gone.

They took a short rest there and learned something the hard way. The trees in the Gloaming can split open and belch out poisoned, sulfurous gas. Lingering in this wood is dangerous, and lingering where the gas seeps is worse. Patched up, they pushed on to Rhydderch's camp. It had not been abandoned. It had been interrupted. The fire was recent. His whole life was sitting there mid-chore with nobody in it.

The water at the camp was wrong. Red, with a sooty red staining along the edges of the stream. That stream was supposed to be one of the last clean sources of water left in the Gloaming. They followed it upstream and found the great alder, split open down its length and pouring the same red poison straight into the water.

Harsk read the ground. Eirlys had been here. Her bootprints ran from Rhydderch's camp to the alder, and then they stopped being bootprints. What left the tree instead was a single wide trail of something heavy being dragged away.

Laeryn whispered quietly a lament that he wished he could help. The tree answered him.

What it told them was worse than they expected. The alder felt responsible. It had stood for centuries deliberately pulling the poison out of the wood around it and holding it in its own bark, taking the sickness so nothing else had to. Then a pair of snakes came to it and made it worse on purpose, pushing more venom into the bark until it was sick enough that word would reach Eirlys. It worked. She came, and they took her, and the tree knew exactly what it had been used for.

They asked about the snakes. Two of them, it said. One small, one large, both venomous, and one of them able to wear a human shape. They dragged Eirlys off before she could put up much of a fight. There is a splash of acid burned into the back of the trunk where she tried.

At that point the party decided they were not going another step in the state they were in, and they were not leaving the tree the way it was either. They worked a bit of circle magic. Indri and Yu put their magic into it, Bringha and Laeryn brought herbal knowledge, and Harsk lent what he had. The combined roll came in over thirty. They did not cure it. They took the edge off, eased what they could, and gave it some peace.

The tree thanked them with a memory. Indri was warned that taking it might be dangerous, because this was the memory of a tree across centuries and not the memory of a person. She took it anyway.

She saw five contraptions in the air. Longboats, more or less, flying, each with a great spearhead at the prow and sailing fins down their flanks. She watched them ram themselves into the hide of a green dragon. The first, then the second, then the third, then the fourth, every one of them shattering apart against her and raining down across the Gloaming while the two forces fought it out overhead. The fifth went in true. It buried itself between the collarbone and the back wing, in the one place she was not armored. Indri watched Death at Sunset come down, the longship and the dragon still turning around each other the whole way, until both of them hit the ground in a clearing a few hours north of the alder, through the untamed part of the wood where no path goes.

And she learned the thing underneath all of it. The carcass is the source. A green dragon's venom, in that quantity, left where it fell, has been seeping into the Gloaming for centuries. The sickening is Death at Sunset, still killing this forest from where she landed.

The memory came with a price. Indri came out of it cursed with a yearning to know more, and she is going to have a hard time doing the practical thing whenever there is something here left to learn.

We leave our heroes at the foot of the great alder, a little healed and a good deal better informed than they were this morning. Eirlys is alive somewhere ahead of them, dragged off by something that can wear a person's face. Indri cannot stop wanting to know. And a few hours north, off every path, the body of a dragon has been poisoning this wood since long before anyone in Glancoed was born.
