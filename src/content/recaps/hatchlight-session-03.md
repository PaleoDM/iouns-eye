We opened at the alder, with a long day already behind them and the hard part still ahead.

Gwernen's spring is the last clean water anywhere north of the alder, and our heroes knew it. They filled every keg and skin they had, took stock of what the morning had cost them, and went north off every path. The crash clearing was a few hours out. Indri was still carrying the alder's memory and the wanting that came with it.

The wood did not cooperate. Harsk read the ground badly on the first go and put them into a bushwhack uphill, working through country that has not had a path in it for centuries. It got worse from there. He led them up onto an owlbear den before anyone quite understood what they were looking at, and the choice was to scale a set of cliffs right there or turn around and give back all the ground they had won. They chose to fall back. The owlbear never knew they were there, but it cost them the better part of two hours, which meant they came into the clearing at nightfall instead of in daylight.

The fifth longship was where the memory said it would be, prow-down in frozen earth, with a furrow beside it road-wide where something vast had dragged itself under. Our heroes spread out and read the clearing before committing to anything. Bringha caught it first. Movement inside the main descent, and shapes in it that were roughly the size and roughly the shape of people.

The plan was sound. Bringha would put a flask of alchemist's fire into the opening to draw whatever was down there up into the light, and everyone else would be hidden and waiting for it. What actually happened was that the fire went in, something came out immediately, and the rest of the party was still finding cover. Bringha was standing alone in front of the breach.

Rhydderch walked straight through the fire and kept coming. He went up like an Everlight Tree and burned for the rest of the fight. Harsk recognized him, and that was the worst of it. They'd shared his camp on the road once. Here he was, posted at the breach with his own snares strung up around him, shepherds and drovers standing to either side of him. Defending a lair like a loyal soldier.

Round one went badly. Bringha was surrounded and dropped to zero while the rest of the dead came around after Harsk and Indri. Round two turned it over. She rolled a natural twenty on her death save, came straight back up, put a very ugly strike into the nearest zombie, and spent the first focus point of the campaign on a Step of the Wind to get out of the middle of them. From there our heroes took the action economy back and never gave it up again. Harsk put an arrow through Rhydderch and put him down. Indri's fire bolts cleaned up behind it. Laeryn and Yu kept everyone upright through the whole of it.

When it was over, Harsk gave him a farewell. Quiet, and short, and the only one Rhydderch was ever going to get. Inside, the ship is built for giants and it never lets you forget it. The cockpit still had both pilots in it, skeletonized, and stacked so precariously that a wrong step looked likely to bring the whole arrangement down on somebody. Past them the hull was torn wide open, and through the tear was not more ship. It was an underground lair.

Getting five people down a rope through a dead giant's cockpit is not a small problem, and Laeryn is the reason it went cleanly. With as much leadership as physical skill, he helped coordinate getting everyone of them down safe and in order.

Indri did not go down with them at first. She stayed up to read the machine, because the curse does not let her leave things alone. Despite the precarious nature, she's a small person and light on her feet, enabling her to dart around the place nimbly. What she got for the risk was a potion of healing tucked where a pilot had kept it within reach, and the black box. The box was garnet once. What was left of it was a coarse garnet sand of disintegrated crystal, and it still held what the crew saw. Indri watched their last minutes.

Four things came out of it. Death at Sunset broke off the fight, and she did not break off because she was beaten. She was protecting a clutch of eggs, and she chose them. The giants brought her down directly over her own lair. But they never actually finished her. And there is one weapon still aboard, an Ancient Fire Giant Harpoon of Dragonslaying, still cocked, still charged, still aimed straight down into the wound.

Indri knows how to fire it. She does not know how to do much of anything else with it.

Below, Yu spotted the little snake before it spotted them. That snake has been ahead of this party since the alder, and it was a corridor away from telling Marilissa and everything under her exactly who had come. Yu killed it where it sat. Nothing below them knew they were coming after that.

Meanwhile, Laeryn got a message through to Eirlys. It was the first contact anyone has had with her since she went into the Gloaming, and she was alive, awake, and sharp enough to know what to do with it. Keep her captor talking, she said, and she would hold the woman's attention for as long as she could. That gave our heroes a clock and a way in.

They found her manacled to the wall and being beaten.

Marilissa had run out of patience. Five days of slow work and wrong answers had gotten her nothing, and her hands were the last thing she had left to try. She has been witch-cursed. She crossed a witch somewhere behind all of this, and the curse took her spellcasting from her.

The fight was dicey on the front end again. Two venomous snakes came in with her, the venom put real damage on the party fast, and Harsk went down. Yu brought him back. Then Marilissa missed, and missed again, and round two went the way round two keeps going for these five. The snakes fell. Laeryn landed the killing blow on Marilissa.

They got Eirlys out of the manacles, and she told them what five days at that bench had taught her. There is a green dragon below. A wyrmling, weeks old, calling himself Sunset-Is-Nigh. He is hers, Death at Sunset's, and he is the one who called Marilissa and made her his. What he has had the two of them working on is a venom that gets through resistance and immunity. He wants a weapon against the things his own blood cannot touch.

We leave our heroes standing in Marilissa's chamber with Eirlys free beside them, low on everything, a long way from anywhere they could safely sleep, and deciding what comes next. Below them is a dragon they are not at all sure they can fight. Above them is a giant's harpoon, loaded, aimed down the wound, and Indri knows which lever fires it.
